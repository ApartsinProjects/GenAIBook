Fine-Tuning Vision and Video Models

Covers task-model training, adapter-based tuning, slice-based evaluation, and interpretation of gains from synthetic mixtures.

Advanced Graduate Engineering Research
Detailed Sections
  1. Representation baselines versus end-to-end task heads using CLIP/OpenCLIP, DINOv2, CNN backbones such as ResNet and EfficientNet, ViT, Swin, DETR-style, SAM 2-assisted segmentation, and Florence-2- or grounding-aware backbones.
  2. Classification, detection, segmentation, captioning, retrieval, tracking, and temporal localization for image and video tasks.
  3. PEFT, LoRA-style adapters, selective fine-tuning, full fine-tuning, multimodal preference-alignment options, distillation options, and training-budget tradeoffs.
  4. Real-only, synthetic-only, repurposed-only, and hybrid mixture experiments with curriculum schedules, sample weighting policies, and replay strategies.
  5. Slice-based evaluation across viewpoint, lighting, motion, occlusion, domain shift, and rare cases.
What This Chapter Covers
  • How to measure whether synthetic data actually improves model utility.
  • How to structure fine-tuning so later deployment remains feasible.
  • How video fine-tuning differs from image-only adaptation.
Deep Dive
  • Inner workings: what changes during adaptation, what remains frozen, and how serving/runtime choices alter final system behavior.
  • Algorithm sketch: training loop, evaluation loop, regression gate, deployment path, and post-launch feedback loop.
  • Intuition: why optimization, deployment, and monitoring are one connected lifecycle rather than separate engineering chores.
  • Tradeoffs: full tuning versus adapters, quality versus latency, quantization versus fidelity, and modular observability versus platform simplicity.
  • Common failure modes: overfitting to synthetic artifacts, slice regressions, quantization regressions, drift, silent retrieval failures, and broken rollback logic.
Illustrations and Figures
  • Training-to-serving lifecycle diagrams, ablation tables, latency-quality tradeoff charts, and drift or failure-bucket dashboards.
Worked Example
  • Run one training or deployment decision from baseline through evaluation, then show the regression checks or monitoring signals that justify the final choice.
Case Study Thread
  • Track one shared lifecycle across the recurring industrial, speech, and incident-review systems so readers can compare adaptation, serving, and monitoring decisions side by side.
Learning Outcomes
  • Fine-tune vision and video models with controlled comparisons.
  • Interpret gains from synthetic mixtures without overclaiming.
  • Connect training strategy to evaluation slices and deployment constraints.

Hands-On Track

  • Run a real-only vs hybrid fine-tuning comparison.
  • Evaluate across rare slices and motion-heavy slices.

Research Track

  • Design a synthetic-data ablation for one vision or video task.
  • Compare representation-first and end-to-end tuning.
Key Tools, Libraries, and Models
  • timm, Transformers, PEFT, Accelerate, TRL for VLM alignment, torchao/torchtune where relevant, distillation recipes, CLIP/OpenCLIP and DINOv2 encoders, CNN backbones such as ResNet and EfficientNet, ViT and Swin backbones, DETR- and Grounding-DINO-style detectors, SAM 2 segmentation workflows, Florence-2 promptable perception patterns, VideoMAE and TimeSformer video backbones, and FiftyOne evaluation views.
Representative Benchmarks and Datasets

Building Multimodal Retrieval, RAG, and Agent Systems

Assembles retrieval, generation, grounding, structured outputs, and temporal reasoning into end-to-end multimodal products.

Advanced Graduate Engineering Research
Detailed Sections
  1. Retrieval systems with shared embeddings, multimodal indexes, vector stores, reranking stages, and document-centric retrieval for PDFs, charts, and slides.
  2. Grounded generation with retrieval, perception, tool use, structured outputs, schema validation, and explanation components.
  3. Chat-with-image, chat-with-audio, chat-with-video, document-question-answering, multimodal RAG, and mixed-modality workflow interfaces.
  4. Temporal reasoning, diarized evidence, and event understanding across audio and video.
  5. System decomposition: modular pipelines, orchestrated tool use, agentic workflows, versus generalist omni interfaces.
What This Chapter Covers
  • How to move from model demos to complete multimodal systems.
  • How retrieval and generation complement each other.
  • How to architect systems that remain debuggable and extensible.
Deep Dive
  • Inner workings: how foundation models combine perception, grounding, retrieval, and generation, and what remains modular versus unified.
  • Algorithm sketch: retrieval-grounding-generation loops, schema validation paths, and modular tool-calling sequences.
  • Intuition: why a decomposed system often beats a monolithic prompt when evidence quality and traceability matter.
  • Tradeoffs: omni-model simplicity versus modular debuggability, retrieval quality versus latency, and flexibility versus governance control.
  • Common failure modes: retrieval miss, OCR corruption, grounding mismatch, invalid structured output, and brittle tool orchestration.
Illustrations and Figures
  • System architecture diagrams, document or multimodal retrieval pipelines, grounding loops, and modular-vs-omni design comparisons.
Worked Example

Case Study Thread

Learning Outcomes

  • Combine retrieval, grounding, and generation in coherent product flows.
  • Choose modular versus generalist architectures based on constraints.

Hands-On Track

  • Implement one audio-video or image-text interaction flow.

Research Track

  • Compare modular and omni-style architectures on one case-study slice.
  • Measure where grounding improves reliability.
Key Tools, Libraries, and Models
  • OpenCLIP, VLMs, omni and any-to-any models, retrieval indices, vector databases or FAISS-style stores, ColPali/ViDoRe-style document retrieval components, FastAPI or Gradio-based service layers, orchestration templates, tool-calling and schema-driven workflows, structured-output validators, and agent runtime frameworks.

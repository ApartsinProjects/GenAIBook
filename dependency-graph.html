Use Cases

  • Self-study: start with Appendices A-F if needed, then follow the core spine before adding one specialization branch.
  • Undergraduate teaching: use the Part I-II-IV-VI route and pull from the appendices as needed.
  • Graduate engineering: add Parts III and V in full, especially Chapters 12-25.
  • Graduate research: add Chapter 8, Chapters 9-13, Chapter 20, Chapter 29, and Appendix F early.

Prerequisite Notes

Where readers can safely branch or defer

  • Chapters 09-13 assume the reader already understands Chapters 01-08 well enough to interpret model choices in relation to task and data design.
  • Chapter 20 is strongest after Chapters 08, 14-19, because it assumes the reader can distinguish training data utility from evaluation utility.
  • Chapters 21-25 assume both the experimental discipline of Part II and the synthetic-data operations of Part IV.
  • Chapter 29 should usually come after at least one full project loop through Chapters 14-25.
  • Appendices A-F are the main self-contained onboarding route for readers with only basic engineering knowledge.
  • Appendix I is useful early for research-oriented readers, but it becomes most concrete after Chapter 08.
